Security teams judge any agent platform against the threat lists they already use. For agents, the list is the OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications, published in December 2025, which names ten risks specific to AI agents.1 Every one of the ten maps to controls a team can put in place and test now.

Every risk on the list has a control

The table maps each risk to the controls that address it in an AI control plane.

The kill switch

Several controls appear in more than one row, and they are the place to start. Approvals bound to the exact action defend against goal hijack and against people trusting a confident draft. One identity per agent, with delegation that narrows at each hop, closes off privilege abuse and limits what a spoofed message between agents can do. Exposure limits that add up across agents and sessions contain both tool misuse and cascading failures.

The threat lists keep growing. Microsoft’s AI Red Team added seven agentic failure modes to its taxonomy in June 2026, among them agentic supply chain compromise, inter-agent trust escalation, session context contamination and MCP and plugin abuse.3 MITRE ATLAS has added agent-specific techniques since December 2025, such as AI Agent Tool Credential Harvesting and Publish Poisoned AI Agent Tool.4 Tagging red-team scenarios with these names lets a team’s results map to the vocabulary other teams use.

What to do next

  1. Developer. Allow-list the tools each agent may call, validate every call against the tool’s schema, and accept only registered, signed and version-pinned MCP servers.
  2. AI Engineer. Bind each approval to the exact action and show the approver the facts behind it, so a polished draft can’t carry a harmful change through.
  3. CISO. Name an owner for what enters the agent registry and another for what approvers are shown, because no gateway closes either risk alone, and tag red-team scenarios with the risk names in the table.
